# Ep. 02 — текст для озвучки (Fish Audio)

Один голос рассказчика. Каждый блок = 10 секунд. Файлы: `ep02_01.mp3`, `ep02_02.mp3` … по номеру клипа.


## INTRO

**01** (0:00)  
(calm) Every day, people tell lies. Small ones. Big ones. Kind ones. (serious) But what if tomorrow… every person on Earth could only tell the truth?

**02** (0:10)  
No white lies. No "I'm fine." No "I'm on my way" when you're still in bed. Everyone says exactly what they think.

**03** (0:20)  
Here are the rules. You can stay silent. But anything you say has to be true. No exaggerating. No fake smiles. No escape.

**04** (0:30)  
Sounds like a better world, right? No scams. No fake news. No cheating. But science says it's way more complicated. Let's live through one day.


## MORNING

**05** (0:40)  
Seven A.M. Mom asks, "Did you sleep well?" And before you can stop it, you say: "No. I watched videos until three A.M."

**06** (0:50)  
But Mom has to be honest too: "Honestly? I'm too tired to be mad." And weirdly… it's the best talk you've had in months.

**07** (1:00)  
Surprise: in one U.S. study, most people told no lies at all that day. Half of all lies came from just five percent of people.


## SCHOOL

**08** (1:10)  
(sly) At school, chaos begins. The teacher asks who did their homework. Your best friend stands up: "I copied mine. From him."

**09** (1:20)  
And the teacher? "To be honest, this lesson bores me too." For the first time ever, the whole class is actually listening.

**10** (1:30)  
Fun fact: kids usually start lying around age two or three. Psychologists see it as a milestone. It means the child understands others have different thoughts.


## LUNCH

**11** (1:40)  
Lunch. Emma sits down next to you. And your mouth just says it: "I've liked you for two years." She says: "I know. Everyone knows."

**12** (1:50)  
All around you, friendships are exploding. "Your jokes aren't funny." "You talk too much." "I only hang out with you for your car."

**13** (2:00)  
Psychologists say small white lies work like social glue. They protect feelings and help people cooperate. Without them, every conversation is a risk.

**14** (2:10)  
Even dating changes. One study found eighty-one percent of online daters lied about their height, weight or age. Tomorrow, every profile tells the truth.


## THE WORLD CHANGES

**15** (2:20)  
After school, you pass a phone store. The salesman just shrugs: "Honestly? It's the same as last year's model. Don't buy it."

**16** (2:30)  
Ads stop working. Billboards can't promise you'll be happier, prettier or richer. Whole industries built on exaggeration start to shake.

**17** (2:40)  
A little kid asks his dad, "Is Santa real?" And the dad has no choice: "No." Some truths… hit harder than others.

**18** (2:50)  
On a TV in the window, a politician admits he has no idea how to fix the problem. The crowd goes silent. Then… they clap.

**19** (3:00)  
And that might be the upside. Economists found that countries where people trust each other more tend to grow richer. Honesty makes every deal faster.

**20** (3:10)  
Then your neighbor runs up, beaming: "We're throwing you a surprise party on Friday!" Okay. Surprises are officially dead.


## THE HARD TRUTH

**21** (3:20)  
(serious) Evening. You visit Grandma in the hospital. The doctor doesn't soften anything. Every number. Every risk. Straight to her face.

**22** (3:30)  
In real life, doctors often soften things. In a 2012 survey, over half of U.S. doctors admitted describing a patient's outlook as better than the facts.

**23** (3:40)  
But Grandma just smiles: "Thank you. Nobody's told me the truth in weeks." Maybe honesty and kindness aren't opposites after all.

**24** (3:50)  
In the hallway you realize: lying is hard work. Brain scans show lying lights up more of the prefrontal cortex, your brain's control center, than the truth.

**25** (4:00)  
And in one Notre Dame experiment, people who cut back on lying for ten weeks reported fewer headaches, less stress and better relationships.


## BACK HOME

**26** (4:10)  
Back home, the news is talking about lie detectors. Funny thing: polygraphs measure stress, not lies. Most U.S. courts won't even accept them.

**27** (4:20)  
Mom sits down and asks how your day was. And for once, you tell her everything. The good. The awkward. The scary. She just listens.

**28** (4:30)  
In your room, your phone buzzes. It's Jake: "Sorry I said your jokes aren't funny. Some of them are. Honestly."

**29** (4:40)  
Then another message. Emma: "Want to get pizza tomorrow?" When everyone tells the truth… you never have to guess.


## ENDING

**30** (4:50)  
(warm) So is a world without lies better? Science says: partly. More trust. Healthier people. But also more hurt feelings… and no surprise parties.

**31** (5:00)  
Maybe the goal isn't brutal honesty. Maybe it's kind honesty. Telling the truth… and choosing how you say it.

**32** (5:10)  
The next morning, the spell is gone. Mom calls: "Did you sleep well?" You say, "Yes!" (whispering) …And it's a lie.

**33** (5:20)  
(friendly) So be honest: would you want to live in that world? Tell me in the comments. And next time: what if you could stop time?
